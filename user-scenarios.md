# User scenarios

All names, organizations, locations, dates, and schedules below are fictional.

## 1. The cross-calendar transportation conflict

The school calendar shows PTA popcorn pickup on Thursday at 3:30 PM. The activity calendar shows swimming at 4:00 PM. Each event looks manageable independently, but travel time makes both impossible.

**Desired outcome:** Surface the conflict early enough for a parent to change pickup, move the lesson, or ask another approved caregiver for help.

## 2. Early dismissal versus a work meeting

A school message announces dismissal at 1:00 PM. A parent's work calendar contains a meeting until 2:30 PM. The usual bus timing no longer solves pickup.

**Desired outcome:** Highlight the changed dismissal time and explain why Friday needs attention.

## 3. A rescheduled activity creates a new overlap

Soccer practice moves from Wednesday to Tuesday. Taekwondo already occurs every Tuesday. The soccer app sends a notification, but it cannot see the recurring taekwondo schedule.

**Desired outcome:** Compare the proposed change with the complete family schedule before approval.

## 4. An action item is buried in a message

A field-trip email includes a permission form due Friday. The form deadline is not a calendar event and can disappear inside a busy inbox.

**Desired outcome:** Propose a task with a due date and require parent confirmation.

## 5. Information is not always an event

A weekly school newsletter mentions several dates, celebrates student work, and includes general announcements. Treating every date as a calendar event would create noise.

**Desired outcome:** Classify uncertain content as information for review rather than automatically creating events.
